02 / Understand the measure

How media items become
weighted visibility.

The study weights institutional items by their estimated potential reach and how prominently the institution appears. It compares that total with potential reach across monitored inflation coverage. This is a constructed weighted share, not the proportion of people paying attention or a simple percentage of articles.

Relative visibility can fall even when institutional coverage stays unchanged, if the wider inflation discussion grows faster. That is a possible mechanism to investigate, not a decomposition established by the headline model.

04 / What this means for HNB

The scale of the result.
The limits of the inference.

−@@SCENARIO@@ percentage points

A lower weighted share when inflation moves from 2% to 10%.

This is the paper’s conditional comparison: hold the model’s other variables fixed and change only inflation. The primary weekly estimate implies a @@SCENARIO@@ percentage-point smaller weighted share (95% interval: @@SCENARIO_CI@@).

That is a change in the constructed share of media coverage, not a @@SCENARIO@@% fall in readership. The chart shows how the estimate changes when trends or year effects are added.

Separate output from relative coverage

The study distinguishes the share of relevant articles from their prominence and reach. More statements or more mentions can coexist with a smaller share of a growing debate. The results do not isolate a single cause of that change.

Paper: Section 4.3 and discussion.

Visibility and centrality are different

Visibility asks how much of the debate the institution occupies. Network centrality asks how centrally it sits among the actors linked through media sources, giving more weight to connections with well-connected actors.

Using common sources, the later monthly centrality trend is @@CENTRALITY@@ index units (95% interval @@CENTRALITY_CI@@; p = @@CENTRALITY_P@@). The interval includes zero: a lasting loss of network position is not established.

Paper: Section 4.7 and Appendix B2; 25 fitted months.

Keep expectations as a separate outcome

For the article-share measure, the baseline weekly projections find no significant future horizons with linear survey interpolation, but find weeks 1–2 with step assignment. The monthly comparison finds month 12. Controls also change the results.

The paper therefore does not establish that a wider media gap causes expectations to become unanchored.

Paper: Section 4.5 and Appendix A12; corrected comparisons.

A practical investigation agenda

What to examine next

  1. Separate institutional coverage from the wider debate. Track the weighted numerator and the inflation denominator alongside the share.
  2. Validate whose voice is being counted. Check HNB-specific, ECB/Eurosystem and ambiguous institutional references separately.
  3. Pair visibility with comprehension. Use audience research to examine what people understand, alongside what they could encounter.
  4. Inspect source participation. Compare source contributions and repeated publication of the same story.
  5. Keep expectations analysis separate. Test survey-timing and model choices before interpreting any predictive relationship.

These are proposals for investigation. The findings do not establish that a particular communication campaign would improve understanding or expectations.

For the governor, the useful distinction is between what HNB publishes, how it appears in the wider debate, and what households understand. The paper measures the middle of that relationship. Its visibility indicator complements communication and survey evidence; it is not a target to maximise.

Why the expectations result depends on survey timing

The survey is monthly. Assigning values to weeks in different ways does not create new weekly responses. A linear assignment moves gradually between monthly values; a step assignment holds a monthly value across its weeks. Results also change with the control set.

How centrality differs from the attention gap

Centrality describes position in the network linking media sources and actors. It is normalised relative to the other actors each month, so changes in network composition can alter the score. This does not establish lasting structural erosion, and the score is not a measure of credibility, trust or policy influence.

06 / Methods & limits

What the indicator can carry.

Return to the figure ↑

Reading the sign

Attention gap =
baseline share − current share

A positive gap means lower visibility than the baseline. A negative gap means higher visibility. Zero marks that reference, not an ideal level of communication.

The displayed monthly baseline is @@BASELINE@@%: the mean weighted share in January–June 2021 after excluding baseline observations more than two standard deviations from the initial mean. Monthly and weekly baselines are calculated separately.

What enters the measure?

The numerator adds potential reach weighted by institutional salience for inflation-relevant items in the HNB corpus. The denominator adds reach across the inflation corpus, plus one. Salience combines title presence, first-mention position, mention frequency and institutional agency cues.

Formal names, acronyms and generic central-bank terms identify the institutional corpus. Generic terms can include other central banks, including ECB or Eurosystem coverage after euro adoption. The measure does not cleanly isolate every HNB message. HNB-specific, ECB/Eurosystem and ambiguous references have not been independently validated as separate categories.

Sample and institutional scope

The analysis covers January 2021–May 2026. The main regressions use 60 monthly or 268 weekly observations. This is a selected vendor corpus, not a complete census of Croatian media or a representative audience sample.

Recorded dates provide the time basis. Generic institutional terms can capture the ECB or other central banks. The indicator is distinct from HNB’s own publication output.

Three kinds of uncertainty

Estimate uncertainty: the reported 95% intervals use the saved Newey–West estimates with Bartlett weights, bandwidth floor(N1/3) and no prewhitening. They describe statistical precision conditional on the selected model.

Model sensitivity: different time controls, media-volume controls and survey assignments can change the estimates. Compare specifications in the evidence section.

Measurement uncertainty: institutional classification, potential reach and vendor sentiment are proxies. The model intervals do not quantify every possible measurement error.

The baseline models include inflation changes, euro adoption, source-reach concentration and vendor negative sentiment alongside the manuscript’s other controls. Equation 9b also includes log HNB-corpus volume. Weekly macroeconomic values are assigned from the month of the week’s start. Public estimates reproduce saved outputs; this publication does not re-estimate the models.

What the study cannot establish

Potential reach is not observed reading, exposure, trust or understanding. Vendor sentiment is not independently validated on this corpus. The gap is neither a count of HNB statements nor a score of communication quality. No threshold for policy-relevant deterioration or monetary welfare cost is established.

HANFA comparisons and instrument diagnostics do not establish an HNB-specific causal mechanism. Forward expectation results depend on interpolation and controls. Media evidence alone cannot establish anchoring of expectations or changes in individual beliefs.

The older paper’s abstract, table and recalculated initial-sample coefficients do not all agree. The extended draft reports this unresolved discrepancy; it does not claim exact legacy replication.
